The When-to-Start Turkey Chart

A 325°F oven, USDA-based timing, and a start-the-bird column so the turkey lands on the table when everyone else does. No scoop physics, no 2 am googling.

Getting started

  1. Find your bird's weight row on page 2 and read the roasting window. If it's stuffed, use the stuffed column (and add a thermometer, the stuffing clock runs slower than the bird's).
  2. The "example start" column assumes dinner at 4:00 pm. For any other dinnertime, add or subtract the difference — the math lives in the box under the chart.
  3. Write your own start time in the blank column, then copy it into the countdown sheet 05 so the two pages agree. They like coordinating.
  4. Page 3 is the trust page: where the thermometer goes, what temperatures count, and what to do when time runs sideways.

The one thing the chart can't do: look inside the bird. Your thermometer can. Time columns are friendly estimates; the thermometer's 165°F is the actual finish line.

The start-time math (in case your dinner isn't at 4)

Pick your bird's unstuffed midpoint from this chart (12–14 lb = 3h 22m; 14–18 lb = 4h; and so on). Subtract it from your dinnertime, then subtract another 25 minutes for resting and carving. Example: dinner at 5:00 pm with a 14 lb unstuffed turkey → 5:00 minus 4:00 minus 0:25 = put the bird in about 12:35 pm. Stuffed birds run about 15 minutes longer — round your start time earlier.

Setup that pays off: rack low-center, pan 2–2.5 inches deep, add ½ cup water to the pan, tuck the wing tips back. A loose foil tent over the breast for the first hour keeps it golden instead of tan, and stays off the legs so they can join the party.

Resting & carving

  • Rest 20–30 minutes under loose foil before carving — juices settle back, and the slices stay on the plate (mostly).
  • Remove ALL stuffing from the cavities right after resting; refrigerate it separately.
  • Thigh first, then breast. Steam is normal at this point; applause is also normal.
  • Turkey slices into smaller pieces chill fastest for leftover storage within 2 hours of dinner. (Sheet 07 handles the labels.)

Surprise scenarios, answered

  • It's throwing ahead of schedule. Pull it early at its 165°F reading, tent it with foil; keep sides on warm hold.
  • It's throwing behind schedule. Foil tent loosely, keep heat at 325°F, and shift side prep to the stovetop. If the gap gets wide, carve the legs early as a warm-up appetizer for the cooks (a proud tradition).
  • It's browning way too fast. Full foil cap over the top for the rest of the roast; keep the thermometer checks coming.
  • The pop-up timer says done but your reading says no? Trust your own thermometer, not the pop-up probe. Check the innermost thigh and the thickest breast yourself.

Kindness note: every experienced cook has a bird story (a 6 pm dinner that became 7:30 pm; a misread pop-up timer). The chart plus the thermometer closes most of them. Yours may live on as the one everyone retells — hopefully with the good ending.
