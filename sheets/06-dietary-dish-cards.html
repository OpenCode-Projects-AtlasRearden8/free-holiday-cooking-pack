The Buffet Dish Cards

Little truth tags for every dish on the table, so nobody has to play ingredient roulette at your buffet — and the cook isn't grilled across the buffet line either. Cut them out on the dashed lines.

Getting started

  1. Give every dish a card. Six cards fit page 2, and page 3 has four spares — one label per dish, tray, and punch bowl.
  2. Check only what's true. If you skip a box because the marshmallow bag was vague, ask your cook — accuracy here protects people more than the card ever could.
  3. Assign utensils: write a number and stick a piece of tape with the same number on the serving spoon. A shared spoon is how the dairy-free dish suddenly isn't.
  4. Update on the spot: with big potlucks, this page works best with the dish list from sheet 01 covered before the buffet opens.

Kind to cooks, kind to guests: "Not sure about cross-contact, homemade in a shared kitchen" is a perfectly honest card line. Guests can make an easier, kinder call with honesty than with a confident wrong answer.

Read-the-label watchlist (store-bought packaging surprises)

  • whey, casein, milk solids — dairy wearing a lab coat
  • soy lecithin, modified food starch — soy or gluten potential
  • gelatin — dessert row (not vegetarian or vegan)
  • "natural flavors" — vague; when in doubt, the person card says "ask me!" for a reason

Cross-contact care: fresh cutting board and knife for allergen-free dishes, washed hands after nuts or crust crumbs, and no shared buffet spoons between dishes. For serious allergies, guests with medical plans make their own plate calls — your job is honest labeling, and you're already doing it.
