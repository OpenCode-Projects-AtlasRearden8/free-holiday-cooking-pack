The Leftover Label Sheets

Twelve pouch- and container-friendly labels, plus the keep-or-toss table that ends every "is this still good?" debate in about four seconds. Fourth-day turkey sandwiches, scientifically enabled.

Getting started

  1. Tape labels onto containers while packing leftovers — a fresh-write label beats memory, every single time.
  2. Write the "use by" date as the math: made on + 3 to 4 days for turkey and sides, gravy is best in 1–2 days, so freeze what you won't finish by then.
  3. The 2-hour rule rules the night: everything settles into the fridge within 2 hours of dinner ending (1 hour if the room runs 90°F+).
  4. Page 3 is the keep-or-toss table: cut it out and tape it inside a cabinet door. It settles debates forever.

Shallow is the secret: leftovers cool fastest (and safest) in small, shallow containers rather than one giant vat. The fridge likes slow beginnings and shallow ends.

Reheat notes:

One honest line for the labels: leftovers that smell questionable, look off, or glow with mystery won't be saved by re-heating anything to 165°F. That's what the freezer is for, and that was always its real job.

The Keep-or-Toss Table (USDA basics)

Refrigerator = 40°F or below. Freezer = 0°F or below (frozen food stays safe indefinitely; these are quality guidelines). Reheat all leftovers to 165°F or until hot and steaming; bring gravies and sauces to a rolling boil.

FoodFridge (40°F)Freezer (0°F)Notes
Cooked turkey meat3 to 4 days3 to 4 monthsslices freeze fastest
Cooked sides & poultry dishes3 to 4 days2 to 4 monthsshallow containers
Stuffing / dressing3 to 4 days2 to 4 monthsstore away from bird
Gravy & pan sauce1 to 2 days (safest)1 to 2 monthsre-boil until rolling
Pumpkin & custard pies3 days (refrigerate)best freshcustard base = fridge home
Fruit pies & breads2 days counter, then fridge1 to 2 monthsloose cover first days
Dinner rolls2 to 3 days counter1 to 2 monthsre-warm in foil
